# George Soros (1930–)

**Known for:** Quantum Fund: ~**30% annualized over three decades**. "The man
who broke the Bank of England" — $1B profit in a day shorting sterling, 1992.

## Core theory: Reflexivity
Markets don't just reflect fundamentals; market prices *change* the
fundamentals (rising stock → cheaper capital → better fundamentals → higher
stock). This feedback loop creates booms and busts that overshoot far beyond
"fair value."

Practical consequences:
- Trends persist longer than rational models expect (ride them).
- The turn, when the loop breaks, is violent (be there for it).
- The best trades are where perception and reality have diverged the most.

## The 1992 sterling trade
- The UK was pegged in the European ERM at an unsustainable rate with a
  recession-bound economy. Defending the peg required rate hikes the UK
  could not afford.
- Soros/Druckenmiller built a ~$10B short — famously, when Druckenmiller said
  he liked the trade, Soros's response was to "go for the jugular": size it
  far larger. Asymmetric payoff: if the peg held, they lose basis points; if
  it broke, they win massively.
- The lesson is **asymmetry + conviction sizing**, not prediction genius.

## Key principles
- "It's not whether you're right or wrong, but how much money you make when
  you're right and how much you lose when you're wrong."
- Survive first: "If I'm losing, I cut back immediately — I don't try to
  recoup. You have to live to fight another day."
- Test hypotheses with small positions; scale hard only when the market
  confirms the thesis.
- Physical tells: he treated back pain as a signal something was wrong with
  the portfolio — i.e., unarticulated risk was accumulating. (The systematic
  version: monitor portfolio-level anomaly metrics, not just per-trade P&L.)

## Lessons for a systematic trader
| Soros principle | Systematic translation |
|---|---|
| Asymmetric payoffs | Prefer setups with capped loss / open-ended win |
| Go for the jugular | Size up only on confirmed, high-conviction signals |
| Cut back when losing | Drawdown-triggered exposure reduction |
| Reflexivity | Momentum/trend signals capture the feedback loop phase |
| Boom-bust overshoot | Don't fade strong trends early; wait for break structure |
