# Richard Dennis & The Turtles (1949–)

**Known for:** Turned ~$1,600 into ~$200M trading commodities. Settled a bet
with partner William Eckhardt — "can trading be taught?" — by training ~20
novices ("Turtles") who went on to earn ~$175M in five years. The clearest
public proof that a **mechanical trend system + discipline** beats talent.

## The Turtle system (fully public since 2003)
A complete, rules-based trend-following system:

### Entry
- **System 1:** Buy a 20-day breakout (price exceeds prior 20-day high);
  short the inverse. Skip the signal if the last S1 trade was a winner.
- **System 2:** Buy a 55-day breakout unconditionally (catches the big trends
  the S1 filter skips).

### Position sizing ("N")
- N = 20-day ATR (Average True Range) of the market.
- Size each unit so **1 N move = 1% of account equity**. Volatile markets get
  small positions automatically; quiet markets get large ones. This is the
  ancestor of modern volatility targeting.

### Pyramiding
- Add 1 unit every ½N of favorable movement, up to 4 units max.

### Stops
- Hard stop 2N from entry for every unit (≈2% account risk per unit).

### Exit
- System 1: exit long on a 10-day low (opposite breakout).
- System 2: exit long on a 20-day low.
- No profit targets — exits only on trend reversal. Winners run for months.

### Portfolio heat rules
- Max 4 units per market, 6 per correlated group, 12 per direction (long/short).

## What actually made it work
- Expectancy math: ~40% win rate, but average win ≫ average loss.
- ATR sizing normalized risk across wildly different markets.
- The hard part was **psychological**: taking every signal after 10 straight
  losses. The Turtles who failed, failed on discipline, not the rules.

## Key insights from Eckhardt
- "The average trader cuts winners and rides losers — the exact opposite of
  what works." Humans are wired for a high win *rate*; profit lives in win *size*.
- Small edges compound: the system's edge per trade was modest; consistency
  over hundreds of trades produced the fortune.

## Lessons for a systematic trader
| Turtle principle | Systematic translation |
|---|---|
| Breakout entries | N-day-high entry gates |
| N-based sizing | ATR/vol-normalized position sizing |
| 2N stop | ATR-multiple stops |
| Opposite-breakout exit | Trailing structural exits, no fixed targets |
| Heat limits | Max positions per sleeve/direction caps |
| Take every signal | Full automation removes the discipline problem entirely |
