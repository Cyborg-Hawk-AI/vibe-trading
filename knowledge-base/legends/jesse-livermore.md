# Jesse Livermore (1877–1940)

**Known for:** The greatest tape-reader of the pre-computer era. Made and lost
several fortunes; his 1929 crash short netted ~$100M (over $1.5B today).
Immortalized in *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* (Edwin Lefèvre).

## Track record
- Made $1M+ in the 1907 panic shorting; J.P. Morgan personally asked him to stop.
- ~$100M profit shorting into the 1929 crash — possibly the largest individual
  trading gain ever relative to the market of the time.
- Went bankrupt at least three times. Died by suicide in 1940. His life is the
  canonical lesson that **method without risk discipline eventually fails**.

## Core method
1. **Trade with the trend of the whole market.** "There is only one side of the
   market... not the bull side or the bear side, but the right side."
2. **Pivotal points.** He waited for a stock to prove itself by breaking a key
   price level (what we now call breakouts) before committing.
3. **Pyramiding into winners.** Start with a probe position; add only when the
   market confirms by moving in his favor. Never average down.
4. **Sit tight.** "It never was my thinking that made the big money for me. It
   always was my sitting." The money is in the major move, not the wiggles.
5. **Cut losses fast.** His probe positions were abandoned quickly when wrong.

## Famous rules (from *How to Trade in Stocks*, 1940)
- Never average losses.
- Markets are never wrong; opinions often are.
- Don't trade every day — trade only when conditions favor you.
- The big money is made in the big swing: catch the trend, not the tick.
- Watch how a stock reacts *after* a move — failure to follow through is signal.

## Why he blew up (equally important)
- Position sizes far beyond any modern risk rule (routinely leveraged to ruin).
- Broke his own rules under emotional pressure (revenge trading, tips).
- No portfolio-level drawdown control — a single wrong macro bet could end him.

## Lessons for a systematic trader
| Livermore principle | Systematic translation |
|---|---|
| Pivotal points | Breakout entry gates (prior N-bar high) |
| Pyramiding winners | Scale-in rules on favorable movement |
| Sit tight | Longer max-holding windows; wide trailing stops in trends |
| Probe positions | Small initial size; add on confirmation |
| Never average down | No adding to losing positions, ever |
| His bankruptcies | Portfolio drawdown breaker is non-negotiable |
