# Linda Bradford Raschke (1959–)

**Known for:** Professional trader for 40+ years across floor and screen;
profiled in *The New Market Wizards*; co-author of *Street Smarts* (with
Laurence Connors) — one of the most respected short-term pattern books ever
written. Her hedge fund ranked 17th of 4,500 for best 5-year performance.

## Core philosophy
1. **Trade market structure, not indicators.** Indicators only describe what
   price/volume already show. Her framework: markets alternate between
   **range contraction and range expansion**, and between trend and chop.
   Identify the current state first; only then pick the play.
2. **Momentum precedes price.** New momentum highs/lows are usually followed
   by a further price extreme after a pullback — so the first pullback after
   a momentum surge is the highest-probability entry in trading.
3. **Every setup is a swing-structure trade.** Defined entry, defined stop at
   structure, first target at the prior swing point. She banks partial
   profits quickly and trails the rest.

## Signature setups (from *Street Smarts*)
- **80-20s:** After a day closing in the top/bottom 10% of its range, fade the
  next day's early continuation — a mean-reversion day trade.
- **Turtle Soup:** Fade a 20-day breakout that immediately fails (named as a
  counter to the Turtles' entry). Captures false-breakout reversals.
- **Holy Grail:** In a strong ADX trend, buy the first pullback to the 20-EMA.
  The most cited "first pullback in trend" formalization.
- **Three Little Indians:** Three ascending push highs = exhaustion pattern.
- **Anti:** A brief counter-trend correction in a young trend, entered as the
  trend re-asserts (slope of fast vs slow stochastic).

## Process discipline
- Preparation is the edge: nightly review, written trade plans, tracking
  personal statistics per setup.
- "The best trades work almost right away." If a trade lingers, risk grows —
  time stops matter.
- Consistency of process beats brilliance: small, repeatable plays, thousands
  of times.

## Lessons for a systematic trader
| Raschke principle | Systematic translation |
|---|---|
| Contraction → expansion | Range-compression detector as pre-entry condition |
| First pullback (Holy Grail) | Trend gate + pullback-to-MA entry trigger |
| Turtle Soup | Failed-breakout reversal as a distinct signal class |
| Best trades work right away | Time stop: exit if not profitable within N bars |
| Per-setup statistics | Per-gene/per-signal attribution in diagnostics |
